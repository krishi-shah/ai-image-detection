\section{Abstract}

AI-image detectors trained on older generative models may fail as newer architectures close the perceptual gap with real photographs. This project fine-tunes an EfficientNet-B3 on CIFAKE (Stable Diffusion v1.4), adds temperature scaling and Grad-CAM explainability, and evaluates transfer to StyleGAN, SD3/Flux, Midjourney~v6, GPT-4o, and Janus-Pro. In-distribution performance is strong (96.96\% accuracy, AUC 0.9971, ECE 0.0026). Under a naive protocol pairing high-resolution generator fakes with low-resolution CIFAKE reals, transfer appears robust (94--97\% fake detection) except for GPT-4o (86.3\%). A resolution-matched control overturns that picture: \textbf{93\% of high-resolution real photographs are predicted FAKE}, and forcing generator fakes to $32\times32$ collapses detection to roughly 35--62\%. The apparent Experiment~3 transfer was largely a \textbf{resolution confound}. Frequency analysis, t-SNE, and Grad-CAM still support a GPT-4o feature-absence story under the native protocol, but the primary methodological finding is that CIFAKE-trained detectors must not be evaluated against high-res fakes without matched-resolution real controls.

\section{Introduction and Motivation}

Detection tools for AI-generated images are typically trained and evaluated on older generators. As Stable Diffusion~3, Midjourney~v6, and GPT-4o produce increasingly realistic outputs, a practical question follows: \textbf{do existing detectors still work, and if not, why?}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Research Question:} \emph{How well do AI-generated image detectors trained on current benchmark datasets generalise to images produced by next-generation generative models, and what accounts for the performance gap?}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Original hypothesis:} Detectors rely on low-level statistical artefacts specific to the training generator; these artefacts are weaker or absent in newer models, producing a measurable generalisation gap that widens with generator novelty.

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Literature Review and Research Gap}

\textbf{Wang et al.\ (2020)} showed ProGAN-trained detectors can transfer across GANs under strong augmentation, but the study predates diffusion models. \textbf{Bird and Lotfi (2024)} introduced CIFAKE and demonstrated $>$92\% detection accuracy. \textbf{Corvi et al.\ (2023)} documented a clear GAN$\leftrightarrow$diffusion gap with frequency-domain separation. \textbf{Gragnaniello et al.\ (2021)} characterised generator-specific fingerprints. \textbf{Guo et al.\ (2017)} proposed temperature scaling. \textbf{Selvaraju et al.\ (2017)} introduced Grad-CAM.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Research gap:} No prior student-scale study combines multi-family chronological evaluation, calibration under shift, comparative Grad-CAM, measurement (not a new detector), and a resolution-matched control for the real-reference protocol.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Architecture}
EfficientNet-B3 (ImageNet pretrained via \texttt{timm}) with classifier \texttt{Linear(1536, 2)}.

\subsection{Training}
AdamW, CosineAnnealingLR ($T_{\max}=20$), CrossEntropyLoss, batch size 32, seed 42. Hyperparameter sweep over 2 learning rates $\times$ 3 weight decays (3 epochs), then full training (20 epochs). Best checkpoint: epoch 2, validation accuracy 0.9694.

\subsection{Calibration}
Temperature scaling learned on validation logits via L-BFGS: $T=1.2189$.

\subsection{Grad-CAM}
Target layer: \texttt{model.conv\_head} (final convolutional head of EfficientNet-B3).

\subsection{Metrics}
Accuracy, AUC-ROC, ECE, F1 (FAKE), fake detection rate (FAKE recall), degradation vs.\ CIFAKE baseline.

\section{Experiment 5: Resolution-Matched Control}
\label{sec:control}

Every REAL image in Experiment~3 is a $32\times32$ CIFAR upscale; every FAKE is a high-resolution downscale. Condition~A uses CIFAKE REAL (native); B uses high-res real photos (native); C forces the same high-res reals to $32\times32$; D forces generator fakes to $32\times32$. Pipeline: \texttt{src/analysis/resolution\_control.py}, notebook~\texttt{07}. $N=300$ per condition; $T=1.2189$.

\textbf{Claim} & \textbf{Outcome} \\

\noindent The primary finding is that a CIFAKE-trained detector uses resolution/resampling as a class cue. Once that confound is removed, generalisation collapses across families.

\section{Limitations}

Single-generator $32\times32$ training; Experiment~3 overall accuracy is not a valid OOD metric under CIFAKE-real pairing; OOD calibration not re-fit; force-$32\times32$ matching is a strong intervention with residual domain shift; GPT-4o spectral story alone does not explain matched-resolution collapse on Midjourney/StyleGAN.

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Conclusions and Future Work}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item CIFAKE-trained EfficientNet-B3 reaches 96.96\% accuracy in-distribution with near-perfect AUC and excellent pre-calibration ECE.
    \item Under the \emph{naive} cross-generator protocol, transfer looked strong except for GPT-4o --- but Experiment~5 shows those numbers were resolution-confounded (93\% of high-res reals called FAKE).
    \item After resolution matching, fake detection falls to roughly 35--62\% across families --- a broad generalisation gap.
    \item GPT-4o remains a hard case under the native protocol; FFT/t-SNE/Grad-CAM support feature absence.
    \item In-distribution temperature scaling is not optimal under shift.
    \item Low-resolution benchmarks must not be evaluated against high-res fakes paired with low-res reals without a resolution-matched control.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Future work:} multi-generator higher-resolution training (e.g.\ GenImage), matched-resolution evaluation, hybrid spatial--frequency features, ensembles, and per-deployment recalibration.
